# Self-playtest · 26 November 2026

## Baseline · v0.1.0

Exploratory browser pass with empty storage and no camera instrumentation. Chromium used software WebGL and normal animation at 1080×680, then 800×500 during the shore walk. The creator already knew the world and its broader objective. This pass is a close critique through ordinary input, with no claim about naïve player behavior.

| Cue and action | Observed result |
| --- | --- |
| The ring and the sunlit water pulled the eye across the basin. Walked toward the nearer standing stone first. | Opening scale held up once the title cleared. The view changed as the bank descended, with near stones and a distant destination in the same frame. |
| Followed the hovering diamond, read the first inscription, then tried walking toward the ring. | The hovering indicator drew attention above the carving. Turning toward the water left the Examine prompt active for the stone, even with the stone outside the frame. E opened a dimmed, blurred journal over the whole landscape. |
| Tried a straight approach toward the far landmark, then turned and followed the land edge. | The desired heading led into the lake. Looking along the shore exposed cliffs, stones and a second marker. Steering took several corrections. The longer route had fewer visual events than the opening. |
| Examined the shore object. | The object read as a small floating post from the bank. The journal described a landing whose steps were mostly below the visible waterline. Two observations were recorded; the exploratory baseline ended here. |

The opening earns its mystery through sight and silhouette. At smaller scale the marker and modal trained attention on collecting notes. The more valuable question is whether the material arrangement gives a reason to look twice. The shoreline detour needs clearer physical evidence before it needs a new region.

## Revision · v0.2.0

Remove hovering site icons and the fixed total from the HUD. Put visual contrast on the objects themselves and require the observer to face the site within a forgiving cone. Show the note alongside the world and leave the full journal available separately. Keep the standing objects solid during examination. Expose sections of the landing above water, place the polished ring on the land-facing side, and introduce a cairn before the headland.

Verification criteria for revisiting the first two sites: looking away clears the prompt, reading keeps the view and movement available, and the shore structure is visible before the text names it. The automated route separately checks all sites, contact with the first stone, journal persistence, and touch input.

Open questions: can a first-time player notice the unmarked shore object or find the headland without supporting copy? The terrain still repeats between landmarks. A useful next experiment would test a change in shoreline shape and foreground framing with an unseen evaluator. The current vertical gesture is mouse or touch only. Software WebGL felt slow during portions of both walks, and these passes provide no hardware performance measurement.

### Replay and iteration

[Rendered comparison frames](evidence/field-observation-comparison.webp) show the baseline and revised first-site interactions from broadly similar viewpoints. These are qualitative examples from the exploratory sessions.

On the revised build I could read the first observation with both the inlaid mark and the lake still visible. A partial turn kept the forgiving prompt while the object remained at the edge of the frame. Turning farther cleared it. The material cue better connects the note to a visible feature, although the raised geometry still reads more like metal inlay than a recess.

An early automated replay timed out crossing the headland under software WebGL. The old 50 ms frame cap discarded movement time whenever rendering took longer. The revision permits up to 160 ms of elapsed movement in collision-checked steps of at most 40 ms. The next trial also showed that exact-coordinate stops for observation prompts were sensitive to key release latency. Use the live prompt for those stops and log coordinates only when diagnosing the automated route. The revised desktop run recorded all three sites, verified collision and water behavior, and verified journal restoration after reload.

The final Chromium smoke suite passed desktop and touch. On touch, the first straight approach exposed the prompt too briefly to tap reliably. A small drag to face the object directly made the prompt stable and also exercised touch look. The extracted production ZIP rendered, recorded the first observation, allowed camera movement while the field note was visible, and reopened it through the journal without browser errors. The numerical time limits used by browser automation are test safeguards.

A separate targeted browser walk reached the revised shore structure. Dry stonework and the ring could be picked out against the water before examining. At the first prompt the ring was low and right of center. The next shore replay should check where the object sits in frame when the prompt first appears and whether players naturally tilt down.
